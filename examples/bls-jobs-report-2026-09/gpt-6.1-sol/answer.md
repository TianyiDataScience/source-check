- **Hiring was nearly flat in September 2026:** Nonfarm payrolls increased by **29,000 jobs**, below the average monthly gain of **45,000** over the prior 12 months. Total payroll employment stood at **159.0 million**.
- **Unemployment edged up to 4.2%**, from **4.1% in August**, with **7.1 million people unemployed**. The rate has stayed between **4.1% and 4.3% since March**.
- **Earlier hiring estimates were revised down by 60,000 jobs:** July’s change went from **+21,000 to −10,000**, and August’s from **+162,000 to +133,000**. July–September job growth averaged approximately **51,000 per month**.
- **Private employers added 46,000 jobs, while government shed 17,000.** All major industries were characterized as showing little monthly change; construction added **11,000**, manufacturing **9,000**, and leisure and hospitality **10,000**.
- **Health care remained a source of growth but slowed:** It added **17,000 jobs**, versus a **33,000** monthly average over the prior year. Ambulatory services added **13,000** and hospitals **12,000**, while nursing and residential care facilities lost **9,000**.
- **Several sectors lost jobs:** Information declined by **10,000**, professional and business services by **9,000**, and financial activities by **7,000**. Financial employment is down **129,000** from its May 2025 peak, including **90,000** jobs lost in insurance-related activities.
- **Wage growth was modest:** Average hourly earnings for private-sector employees rose **5 cents, or 0.1%, to $37.81**, up **3.0% over the year**. Production and nonsupervisory workers’ earnings rose **7 cents, or 0.2%, to $32.60**.
- **Hours were steady:** The average private-sector workweek remained **34.4 hours**. Manufacturing averaged **40.6 hours**, including **3.0 hours of overtime**; average private-sector weekly earnings reached **$1,300.66**.
- **Labor-force participation ticked up:** The participation rate reached **61.8%**, from **61.6%**, and the employment-population ratio reached **59.2%**, from **59.1%**. The household survey estimated **406,000 more employed people**, though the report characterized the change as small.
- **Labor-market challenges persisted despite lower broad underutilization:** Black unemployment rose from **6.0% to 7.0%**; teenage unemployment was **14.5%**. Long-term unemployment totaled **1.9 million**, or **27.1% of all unemployed people**, and **4.5 million** worked part time for economic reasons. The broader **U-6 underutilization rate fell to 7.6%**, from **7.7%**.
