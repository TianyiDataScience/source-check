Here is a 10-point summary of the September 2026 Employment Situation report, highlighting the most important numbers and trends:

*   **Sluggish Job Growth:** The U.S. economy added just 29,000 nonfarm payroll jobs in September, falling short of the average monthly gain of 45,000 seen over the previous 12 months.
*   **Unemployment Holds Steady:** The overall unemployment rate saw little change, settling at 4.2% with 7.1 million people unemployed. The rate has hovered in a tight range between 4.1% and 4.3% since March.
*   **Prior Months Revised Downward:** Job numbers for July and August were revised downward by a combined 60,000 jobs. July was revised from +21,000 to -10,000 (a loss of jobs), and August was revised down from +162,000 to +133,000.
*   **Wage Increases:** Average hourly earnings for private-sector employees edged up by 5 cents (0.1%) to $37.81. Over the past 12 months, hourly wages have increased by 3.0%.
*   **Steady Workweek Hours:** The average workweek for all employees on private nonfarm payrolls remained completely unchanged at 34.4 hours. 
*   **Labor Force Participation:** The labor force participation rate stayed relatively flat at 61.8%. The employment-population ratio was also stable at 59.2%, showing virtually no net change since January.
*   **Healthcare Leads Job Gains:** The healthcare sector continued to add jobs (+17,000), driven by ambulatory services and hospitals. However, this growth has decelerated compared to its 33,000 average monthly gain over the prior year. 
*   **Modest Gains in Construction & Manufacturing:** Construction added 11,000 jobs, while manufacturing added 9,000. Manufacturing is now up 72,000 jobs since hitting a recent low in December 2025.
*   **Financial Sector Struggles:** Financial activities lost 7,000 jobs in September. The sector has shed 129,000 jobs since reaching a peak in May 2025, heavily driven by job losses among insurance carriers. 
*   **Underemployment Remains Flat:** The number of long-term unemployed (jobless for 27+ weeks) stayed essentially unchanged at 1.9 million. Additionally, 4.5 million people are working part-time for economic reasons, meaning they want full-time work but cannot find it or had their hours reduced.
